# Shared honeymoon travel

Run `supabase/shared-travel.sql` after the installed wedding-project and collaboration access setup. A ready-to-run copy is `C:/Users/ayeni/Downloads/cobridey-shared-travel.sql`. This adds creator-owned trips and separately revisioned itinerary/reservation records without changing existing private honeymoon records. No provider account or new secret is needed.

Open **Honeymoon Planning & Travel → Plan with partner**. Authorized wedding planners/partners/couples can create their own trip, initially visible only to its creator. The creator selects accepted wedding members as viewers or editors. Project managers, the wedding owner and other collaborators do not automatically see someone else's private trip. Selected collaborators can open their shared travel plans from the existing collaborator workspace or a copied trip link. A link alone never grants access.

Only the creator controls sharing and archival. Editors can update shared trip details and itinerary items; viewers can read and export. Current project membership is checked on every read/write, so removing membership or explicit trip access revokes it. Archived trips remain available to the creator for restoration but disappear from collaborators. Raw travel/history/write tables and internal helpers have no client grants. Private travel documents, passport data, other honeymoon records, personal budgets and financial records are not copied into this workspace. Shared notes and reservation references are intentionally visible to the people selected for that trip.

Flights, stays, transfers, experiences, meals and travel tasks record explicit local start/end times and separate IANA time zones. PostgreSQL converts them to UTC, rejects nonexistent daylight-saving times, validates positive duration and trip-date bounds, and preserves valid date-line crossings. Ambiguous fall-back wall times use PostgreSQL's standard-time interpretation; exports reflect the saved UTC instants. Main trip time-zone changes do not reinterpret existing activities. Narrowing trip dates rejects active activities outside the proposed dates. Complete is rejected for a future non-task activity.

Booked/confirmed activities that reserve itinerary time cannot overlap another booked/confirmed reserved activity in that trip. Stays and parallel plans can be marked as not reserving time. Planned overlaps remain visible as conflicts for review. Cancelling an activity releases its reserved time while preserving history. Per-trip row locks serialize edits; expected trip/item revisions and exact request IDs protect against stale devices and interrupted requests. Limits are 100 trips per creator/wedding, 2,000 itinerary records per trip, a one-year trip/duration window and up to 100 accepted recipients per access level.

Provider names, HTTPS links and reservation references are manually recorded confirmations, not live bookings, availability, payments, travel advice or calendar synchronization. Calendar export refreshes authorized records before generating UTC ICS events and omits notes, provider links and booking references. Exported calendars contain only activity titles, locations and times. Existing private honeymoon plans continue to save independently through account cloud storage; no automatic sharing/import is performed.

Tests cover default privacy even from an uninvited planner, explicit viewer/editor access, creator-only sharing, membership removal, raw-table denial, exact retries, revisions, narrowing dates, overlap/cancellation, overnight/date-line flights, invalid zones and DST gaps, mobile creation/editing controls and UTC calendar content without reservation references. Production build and portal review are checked. Live account/device testing remains deferred by the user.
